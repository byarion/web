Income tax in Spain (IRPF)

The IRPF (Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas) is Spain's personal income tax. It is the tax you pay on what you earn during the year, and the more you earn, the higher the percentage you pay.

Quick answers

How much income tax do you pay in Spain?

It depends on your income, your region and your personal situation. Tax residents pay between 19% and 47% on each slice of income (see the brackets below), but the average rate is much lower than the top bracket. For a typical salary of €30,000 gross, our salary calculator estimates about 16% of the gross amount.

Who pays income tax in Spain?

Tax residents pay on their worldwide income. Non-residents pay only on income earned in Spain, with a different tax (see below).

When do you file the tax return?

The campaign usually runs from early April to the end of June, for the previous calendar year. Details in our guide to filing your first income tax return.

How it works: it is progressive

"Progressive" means that income is split into slices (brackets) and each slice is taxed at its own rate. You pay the higher rate only on the part above each bracket, never on your whole salary.

Spain's income tax brackets (general scale)

These are the typical rates that combine the national and the regional part for general income, such as salary. Each region under the common regime can adjust its own part, so the exact figures vary a little.

Taxable incomeRate
Up to €12,45019%
€12,450 to €20,20024%
€20,200 to €35,20030%
€35,200 to €60,00037%
€60,000 to €300,00045%
Over €300,00047%

Savings income (interest, dividends and gains from selling investments) is taxed on a separate scale that starts at 19% and rises to 30% for very large amounts.

A worked example

Imagine a taxable income of €30,000. The brackets are applied slice by slice:

  • €12,450 × 19% = €2,365.50
  • €7,750 × 24% = €1,860.00
  • €9,800 × 30% = €2,940.00

That adds up to €7,165.50. Then the tax on your personal minimum (€5,550 × 19% = €1,054.50) is subtracted, because that part of your income is not taxed. The result is about €6,111, or 20.4%. That is the average rate, not the top bracket of 30% that applies to the last slice.

Note that "taxable income" is not your gross salary: Social Security contributions and a standard allowance for work expenses are deducted first. That is why the salary calculator shows a lower percentage for a €30,000 gross salary.

Tax residents and non-residents

  • Tax residents: in general, you are a tax resident if you spend more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year, or if your main base of activities or economic interests is in Spain. You pay the brackets above on your worldwide income.
  • Non-residents: you pay a separate tax (IRNR) only on income earned in Spain, generally at 19% for residents of the EU or EEA and 24% for the rest, with no personal allowances in most cases.
  • Special regime for newcomers: some people who move to Spain for work can opt for a special regime with a flat 24% on employment income up to a limit. It has strict conditions, so check it with an adviser before relying on it.

Withholding on your payslip

During the year, your employer deducts part of your IRPF from each payslip and pays it to the tax authority for you. It is called withholding. It is an advance on the tax, not the final tax. See how it appears in how to read your payslip.

The annual tax return

Each year, the previous year's return is filed and the withholding you already paid is compared with the tax you actually owe:

  • You get a refund: if too much was withheld.
  • You have to pay: if too little was withheld.

Whether you must file depends on how much you earn and how many payers you have. As a rough guide, the limit is around €22,000 a year with a single payer and lower with several payers, but these limits and the exceptions change, so check them every year.

Allowances and deductions

Part of your income is not taxed because of your personal circumstances (the personal and family minimum), and there are deductions and reductions that lower the tax, for example for children, for rent in some regions or for contributions to certain plans.

An important exception: Basque Country and Navarre

In the Basque Country and Navarre, income tax is regulated and managed by the regional (foral) tax authorities, with their own rules, brackets and deadlines. So the rules above can differ there.

If you are self-employed

Self-employed people pay income tax through quarterly advances, on top of other payments. We explain it in self-employed taxes in Spain. Tax on interest, dividends and gains is covered in how investments are taxed in Spain.

Frequently asked questions

What is the income tax rate in Spain?

There is no single rate. For employment income the brackets go from 19% to 47%, applied slice by slice. Your average rate is lower than your top bracket.

Is there a Spanish tax calculator?

Yes. Our salary calculator estimates Social Security, income tax and your net pay from your gross salary, with 12 or 14 payments.

Do I pay tax twice if I also pay tax in another country?

Often there is a double taxation treaty between Spain and the other country that prevents it. The rules depend on the countries involved, so check the treaty or ask an adviser.

In short

The IRPF is the tax on your income: progressive (19% to 47%), advanced through payslip withholding and settled in the annual return. Your average rate is lower than your top bracket.

This guide is a general explanation, not tax advice. Rates, limits and rules change, and regions differ. For your own case, check with the tax authority that applies to you or an adviser.
