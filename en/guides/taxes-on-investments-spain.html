How investments are taxed in Spain

When your money earns money (interest on a deposit, dividends from a share or the gain when you sell a fund), that income is taxed under the IRPF. Here is how it works, with worked examples.

Quick answers

How much tax do you pay on interest and dividends in Spain?

Between 19% and 30%, depending on the amount. Interest, dividends and gains from selling investments go into a special part of the tax return called the savings base, with its own brackets.

Do you pay tax on a fund if you do not sell?

No. The gain is only taxed when you sell or redeem. While you stay invested, the value can rise and fall without you paying anything.

What if I move my money from one fund to another?

In Spain, a transfer between investment funds does not trigger tax at that moment: the tax is deferred until you finally redeem. This does not apply to buying and selling listed ETFs.

The savings brackets

Savings income is added up and split into brackets, like salary, but with lower percentages:

Savings incomeRate
Up to €6,00019%
€6,000 to €50,00021%
€50,000 to €200,00023%
€200,000 to €300,00027%
Over €300,00030%

For most small savers this means paying 19% or 21%.

What counts as savings income

  • Interest from deposits, savings accounts and bonds (the coupon). See fixed deposit vs savings account and bonds and fixed income.
  • Dividends from shares and some funds.
  • Capital gains when you sell shares, ETFs or index funds for more than you paid.

Withholding: an advance payment

Banks and fund managers usually withhold 19% of interest, dividends and gains when you redeem a fund, and pay it for you. It is an advance, just like payslip withholding. In the tax return it is compared with what you actually owe (see income tax in Spain).

Example 1: a deposit

You put €10,000 in a deposit at 3% a year:

  • Interest for the year: 10,000 × 3% = €300.
  • 19% withholding: 300 × 0.19 = €57.
  • You receive net: 300 − 57 = €243.

Example 2: selling a fund at a gain

You invest €10,000 in an index fund and years later sell it for €12,000:

  • Gain: 12,000 − 10,000 = €2,000.
  • Tax at 19% (if it is your only savings income of the year and falls in the first bracket): 2,000 × 0.19 = €380.

The figures are illustrative: they ignore fees and simplify your real situation.

Losses count too

If you sell at a loss, that loss can offset gains and reduce your tax, within limits and time frames. There are also special rules, for example if you buy the same product again shortly after selling at a loss. Check the conditions when you file.

Basque Country and Navarre

The regional (foral) tax authorities have their own rules and rates, so the percentages can differ there.

Where you declare it

All of this goes into the annual tax return. If it is your first time, read how to file your first income tax return.

In short

Interest, dividends and gains are taxed in the savings base, between 19% and 30%. You pay when you sell, not for holding the investment. A transfer between funds defers the tax.

This guide is a general explanation, not tax advice. Rates and rules change and depend on your case and region. Check with the tax authority that applies to you or an adviser.
