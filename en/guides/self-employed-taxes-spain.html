Self-employed taxes in Spain (autónomo)

Working as an autónomo (self-employed) in Spain means managing several payments yourself: the Social Security contribution, VAT and income tax. Here is how each fits in, with an invoice example.

Quick answers

What taxes does a self-employed person pay in Spain?

Mainly income tax (IRPF) on their profit, VAT (collected from clients and paid over each quarter) and the autónomo contribution to Social Security, which is not a tax but is a compulsory monthly payment.

How often are the taxes filed?

Usually every quarter: VAT (form 303) and the income tax advance payment (form 130). Once a year, the annual tax return. The deadlines are usually the first 20 days of the month after the quarter, and the last quarter runs to the end of January.

What is the advance payment?

It is an advance of your income tax. Under direct estimation it is usually 20% of accumulated net income, minus earlier payments and any withholding already applied to your invoices.

The three pieces

  • Autónomo contribution: paid monthly to Social Security. The amount depends on your real net income, in brackets, and is updated every year. For your case, use the official Social Security simulator.
  • VAT: you add VAT to your invoices (see what VAT is) and each quarter you pay over the difference between the VAT you collected and the VAT you paid on deductible expenses.
  • Income tax: you pay on your profit, with the brackets explained in income tax in Spain.

What is net income

It is what is left after subtracting deductible expenses from your income: those needed for your activity and backed by an invoice. The autónomo contribution also counts as an expense. Income tax is calculated on that net income.

Withholding on invoices

If you invoice companies, you sometimes have to deduct an income tax withholding on the invoice: normally 15%, or 7% in the first years of activity if you meet the conditions. Your client pays it to the tax authority for you, as an advance on your income tax. Not every self-employed person has to apply it: it depends on the activity and the client.

Example with one invoice

You bill €1,000 of professional services to a company, with 21% VAT and 15% withholding:

  • VAT: 1,000 × 21% = €210.
  • Income tax withholding: 1,000 × 15% = €150.
  • Invoice total: 1,000 + 210 − 150 = €1,060.

You collect €1,060. But note: the €210 of VAT is not yours, you will pay it over to the tax authority in the quarter, and the €150 withholding is already an advance of your income tax. Your real income is the €1,000 base, before expenses and taxes.

Common mistakes

  • Spending the VAT you collected. It is not yours: it has to be paid over.
  • Not keeping invoices for expenses: without them they cannot be deducted.
  • Not setting money aside for taxes. Many self-employed people reserve part of each payment for the quarterly filings. An emergency fund also helps in slow months.

Basque Country and Navarre

The regional (foral) tax authorities have their own forms, rates and deadlines, so the above can differ there.

In short

A self-employed person pays the Social Security contribution, pays over VAT each quarter and advances income tax through quarterly payments and invoice withholding. The annual return settles everything.

This guide is a general explanation, not tax advice. Rates, deadlines and conditions change and depend on your activity and region. For your case, check with the tax agency or a gestoría.
