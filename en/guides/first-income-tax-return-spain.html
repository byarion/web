How to file your first income tax return in Spain

Filing an income tax return for the first time can be intimidating, but in most cases it is a fairly guided process: the tax authority already knows much of your information and offers you a draft. Your job is to review it and confirm it.

What it is and what it is for

It is the moment to settle last year's accounts with the tax authority. During the year, your employer has been deducting part of your income tax from each payslip (the withholding). The tax return works out how much tax you really owed and compares it with what you have already advanced. To understand the tax itself, see the guide Income tax in Spain (IRPF).

Do I have to file?

In general, you must file if during the year you earned more than €22,000 gross from work with a single payer. The limit drops to €15,000 if you have more than one payer and the second and later ones add up to more than €1,500. There are other cases (for example, certain investment income or capital gains), so check on the Tax Agency's website.

Even if you are not obliged, it may be worth filing if your employer withheld more than necessary: the tax authority will refund the difference.

When to file

The campaign usually opens in April and ends in late June. The exact dates, including phone and in-person help, are published every year, so check the official website.

Important if you live in the Basque Country or Navarre

The Basque Country (with the provincial treasuries of Álava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa) and Navarre have their own tax regime. You file with your provincial treasury, not with the state Tax Agency, and the rules, deductions and dates can differ from what this guide describes. The underlying concepts (income, withholding, result) are the same, but always check with your own authority.

Step by step to file

  1. Log in with your identification. You can use Cl@ve, a digital certificate or a reference number. This is the hardest part the first time, so do it with time to spare.
  2. Download your draft. The tax authority already has data sent by employers and banks: your payslips, interest and other income. It uses them to prepare a proposal.
  3. Review it carefully. Check that income and withholdings match your payslips or your employer's withholding certificate. An error you do not correct is an error you sign.
  4. Add what is missing. Your personal and family situation (children, disability, dependants) and any deductions that apply, including regional ones where relevant.
  5. Look at the result. Before confirming, the application shows whether you owe, get a refund or end at zero.
  6. Confirm and file. Always keep the proof of filing.

The three possible results

  • To pay: less was withheld than you owed and you must pay the difference. You can set up a direct debit or, in general, split it into two instalments.
  • Refund: too much was withheld and the tax authority returns the difference. It usually takes a few weeks or months, with a maximum of six from the end of the deadline.
  • Zero: what you advanced matches what you owed.

Documents and data to have at hand

  • The withholding certificate or the year's payslips.
  • Details of any extra income: rent, interest, sale of investments or of a property.
  • Proof of anything you want to deduct (for example, donations or pension plan contributions).
  • Your family details, if they affect your personal and family allowances.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Accepting the draft without checking it. Some income or personal circumstance may be missing.
  • Forgetting income from a second payer. It is a common reason why the tax authority later asks for a difference.
  • Leaving it to the last day. If there is a problem with access or with your account, you will have no margin.
  • Not keeping the supporting documents. The tax authority can ask for them for several years.
  • Mixing territories. If you are a resident of a foral territory, do not use the state procedure.

When to ask for help

If you have income from several sources, rent, investments, sold a property or are self-employed, a tax adviser or the tax authority's help desk can save you mistakes. For a simple situation (a single payslip), many people do it on their own in half an hour.

In short

The tax return is a settling of accounts with the tax authority: review the draft, check income and withholdings, and keep the supporting documents. If you live in a foral territory, follow your provincial treasury's rules.
