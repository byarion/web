Index funds for beginners

An index fund is an investment fund that copies an index, such as the S&P 500 or the MSCI World, instead of trying to pick the best shares. This is called passive management.

How it works

If the index has 500 companies, the fund buys those 500 in the same proportions. If the index rises 7%, the fund rises roughly 7%, minus fees. With your money, you buy a small piece of all those companies at once.

Why they became popular

  • Diversification: with a single fund, your money is spread across hundreds or thousands of companies.
  • Low fees: there is no team picking shares, so they usually charge far less than actively managed funds.
  • Simplicity: you do not need to analyse companies one by one.

Why fees matter

A fee looks small, but it is charged every year. On €10,000 invested, a 0.2% fee is €20 a year, and a 1.5% fee is €150. Over the years, with compound interest, the difference is large.

What the risks are

  • It can fall: if the market drops, the fund drops with it, sometimes for years.
  • No guaranteed return: past performance does not ensure future results.
  • It needs time: it is usually thought of as long term, not for money you need soon.

In Spain

Investment funds allow you to move your money to another fund without paying tax until you sell. Taxes depend on each case, so it is worth reading up or asking a professional.

Index fund and ETF

An ETF is another way to copy an index, but it is bought and sold on the stock market like a share. Both aim at the same thing, with differences in how they are bought and taxed.

In short

An index fund copies an index, spreads your money across many companies and charges low fees. It can lose value and is meant for the long term.
