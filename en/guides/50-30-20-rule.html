The 50/30/20 budgeting rule

The 50/30/20 rule is a very simple method to organise your money: you split what you earn each month into three big parts.

The three parts

  • 50% for needs: housing, food, utilities, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments.
  • 30% for wants: leisure, restaurants, travel, subscriptions, purchases that are not essential.
  • 20% for saving and investing: emergency fund, saving for goals and extra debt payments.

It is applied to your net pay, that is, what you actually receive after taxes.

An example

If you take home €1,800 a month:

  • €900 for needs.
  • €540 for wants.
  • €360 for saving and investing.

How to start

  1. Write down everything you spend for a month.
  2. Classify each expense as a need or a want.
  3. Compare your real split with 50/30/20.
  4. Adjust gradually, starting with saving: move it to another account right after payday.

If the numbers do not work

The rule is a guide, not a law. If you live somewhere with expensive housing, your needs may go above 50%. You can adapt it, for example to 60/20/20, or start with a smaller saving, even 5%, and raise it over time.

How much your savings grow

With the savings calculator you can see how much you would have in a few years by saving a fixed amount every month. And if you do not have a cushion yet, start with the emergency fund.

In short

Split your net pay into 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings, and adapt it to your situation.
