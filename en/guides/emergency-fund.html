Emergency fund

An emergency fund is money you set aside for surprises: a breakdown, an unexpected bill or losing your income for a while. It is the foundation of calm finances.

How much to save

The usual rule is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses (housing, food, utilities, transport, debt payments). For example, if your basic costs are €1,200 a month, your fund would be between €3,600 and €7,200.

If your income varies, as with a freelancer, many people prefer a bigger cushion of 6 to 12 months.

Where to keep it

What matters is that it is accessible and safe. You should be able to use it quickly without risk of losing part of it.

  • A savings account separate from your everyday one, so you do not spend it by accident.
  • If it pays some interest, better, but the priority is being able to withdraw it when you need it.
  • It is not a good idea to keep it in investments that could fall just when you need the money.

How to build it step by step

  1. Work out how much you spend each month on essentials.
  2. Set a small first goal, such as one month of expenses.
  3. Set up an automatic transfer on payday, even a small amount.
  4. Raise the goal gradually until you reach your target.

What counts as an emergency

An emergency is something unexpected and necessary: a repair, a medical urgency or losing your job. Holidays or sales are not. If you use the fund, refill it as soon as you can.

Why it comes before investing

Without a cushion, a surprise can force you to sell investments at a bad time or take out an expensive loan. That is why it is usually the first step.

In short

Keep 3 to 6 months of essential expenses somewhere safe and easy to reach, and only touch it for real emergencies.
