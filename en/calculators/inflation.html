Inflation calculator

Indicative result. Not financial advice.

How it is calculated

Inflation builds up year after year, like compound interest. To know what something that costs a given amount today will cost in n years, multiply by (1 + inflation)n.

Example: with 3% inflation for 10 years, something that costs €1,000 today will cost about €1,344. In reverse, €1,000 kept earning nothing will buy what about €744 buys today.

Why it matters

Leaving money idle makes it lose purchasing power every year. That is why it is worth looking at returns after inflation. To understand the concept better, read the guide What is inflation?.

Real inflation changes every year. This tool assumes a constant figure, so it is only an estimate.

How to use the calculator step by step

  1. Enter the amount you want to analyse.
  2. Enter the annual inflation you assume (for example, the latest CPI).
  3. Choose the years.
  4. You will see what today's amount will cost in the future and how much purchasing power your money will have.

What the CPI is

The CPI (Consumer Price Index) measures how the prices of a typical basket of goods change. In Spain it is published by the INE, and it is the usual reference when talking about inflation. See also the guide what inflation is.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking that a small inflation «does not matter»: over the years the effect builds up a lot.
  • Comparing the return on savings without subtracting inflation.
  • Assuming inflation will be constant: the real one changes every year.

Frequently asked questions

What inflation rate should I use?

You can use the latest annual CPI figure published by the INE, or any number you want to simulate, such as 2% or 3%.

How much does my money lose with 2% inflation?

Each year it buys roughly 2% less. Over 10 years, €1,000 will buy what about €820 buys today.

What will €1,000 be worth in 20 years with 3% inflation?

It will buy what about €554 buys today. The other way round, what costs €1,000 today will cost about €1,806.

What is purchasing power?

It is how much you can buy with your money. If prices rise and your money does not, your purchasing power falls.
