How compound interest works

With compound interest, the interest you earn is added to your capital and, the following month, earns interest too. Over time the effect speeds up: each year you earn on a larger amount.

You can choose the compounding (daily, monthly or yearly). The default is monthly. The monthly contribution is paid in at the end of each month.

Final amount = starting × (1 + i)n + contribution × ((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i

Where i is the monthly return and n the number of months.

Three examples

  • Starting amount only: €10,000 at 5% a year for 20 years grows to about €27,126, with nothing added.
  • Contributions only: €200 a month at 5% for 20 years adds up to about €82,207, of which €48,000 is what you put in.
  • Both: €5,000 to start and €150 a month at 6% for 15 years gives about €55,893, with €32,000 contributed.

Keep in mind

The return is an assumption: no investment guarantees a fixed percentage, and those that can earn the most can also lose. The result also ignores inflation, fees and taxes, which reduce what you actually keep.

How to use the calculator step by step

  1. Enter the initial capital and the monthly contribution (it can be 0).
  2. Enter the annual return you want to simulate.
  3. Choose the years and the compounding (daily, monthly or yearly).
  4. See the final money, what you put in yourself and what interest has generated, year by year.

Daily, monthly or yearly compounding: what changes?

Compounding tells you how often interest is added to the capital. The more often, the sooner that interest starts earning more interest. With €10,000 at 5% a year for 20 years and no contributions:

CompoundingFinal money
Yearly€26,533
Monthly€27,126
Daily€27,181

The difference between monthly and daily is small. Return, time and fees matter far more. When comparing products, look at the TAE (APR), which already includes the effect of compounding.

The daily compound interest formula

Final capital = initial × (1 + r ÷ 365)365 × years

Where r is the annual return as a decimal (5% = 0.05). With monthly contributions, the calculator uses the effective monthly rate equivalent to the chosen compounding.

Frequently asked questions

What return should I enter?

It is a figure you choose to simulate. Try several (for example 2%, 5% and 7%) to see how the result changes. There is no safe number.

What matters more, contributing more or waiting more years?

Usually time. Starting earlier gives interest more room to earn interest. Change the years and the contribution to see it for yourself.

Why does it not match my bank or fund?

Real products have fees, taxes and returns that change every year. This calculator uses a fixed, constant percentage.

How is daily compound interest calculated?

The annual rate is divided by 365 and applied every day. For example, €5,000 at 4% for 3 years gives 5,000 × (1 + 0.04 ÷ 365)1,095, about €5,637.

Is daily compounding better than monthly?

It gives slightly more money, but the difference is small. What matters is the rate, the term and whether fees eat into the return.
